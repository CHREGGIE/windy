# Activity rules: research brief

Roofing, recreational boating, golf, drone flying. Crane appears only for comparison.
Written November 2026 to be reviewed by the owner and the team. Nothing here is built.

## How to read this

- **Sourced**: a source is linked. Most links came from web search results. The session could not
  open weather.gov, ecfr.gov or osha.gov directly, so a few rule texts were read through mirrors
  (Cornell LII, NWS office pages, trade sites). Check them against the primary page before anyone
  quotes them in the app.
- **Unverified**: recalled, inferred, or only found on a secondary site. Confirm before relying on it.
- **No source found**: we looked and found nothing. The brief proposes no number in these cases.
- Hard and soft limits:
  - **Legal**: law or regulation.
  - **Spec**: a manufacturer limit for the user's own equipment or material.
  - **Official**: an NWS product definition (an advisory or warning).
  - **Guidance**: a safety body or trade association recommends it.
  - **Convention**: a common habit with no authority behind it.

Every proposed rule follows the product principles: the worse source wins, no data means `UNKNOWN`,
caution starts at 80% of a user limit, a word sits beside every colour, and the result is a
planning aid, not a safety clearance.

## 1. Roofing

### 1.1 What decides it, ranked

1. **Lightning or thunderstorms.** OSHA and NOAA say to get to a safe place at the first thunder, not
   to start a task you cannot stop quickly, and to wait 30 minutes after the last thunder
   ([OSHA FS-3863, hosted by NWS](https://www.weather.gov/media/owlie/OSHA_FS-3863_Lightning_Safety_05-2016.pdf);
   [OSHA 3863](https://www.osha.gov/Publications/OSHA3863.pdf)). Employers are expected to put a lightning
   procedure in the emergency action plan (29 CFR 1926.35).
2. **Wind gusts, mainly while handling sheet goods.** Plywood, OSB, rolls and panels catch wind like a sail.
3. **Rain and wet surfaces.** Slipping is the risk to people. Products also need a dry deck: Carlisle's
   self-adhered underlayments are applied only "when the deck is dry"
   ([Carlisle WIP 300HT](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0268/6153/5407/files/CarlisleWIP-300HT-Sell-Sheet.pdf)).
4. **Cold, for materials.** Asphalt shingle seal strips are activated by heat. In cold weather they need
   hand sealing, and a seal failure from too little heat is not a manufacturing defect
   ([GAF cool-weather tips](https://www.gaf.com/en-us/blog/residential-roofing/cool-weather-roofing-must-know-tips-for-installing-shingles-in-cool-temperatures-281474980457570);
   [GAF R-114 hand sealing](https://www.gaf.com/en-us/document-library/documents/technical-bulletins-&-notes/r-114-hand-sealing-shingles.pdf)).
5. **Heat, for people.** Heat illness on an exposed roof.
6. **Frost, dew, ice, snow.** Slip hazard. Ice also blocks sealing.
7. **Daylight.**

they are installing, which sets the minimum temperature. No trade-wide default exists. Pre-filling 35 mph

mph and °F (US). Roofers talk about "gusts", "too windy to lay sheets", "seal down", "hand-tab" or
"hand-seal", and "dry-in". **Unverified**: the trade words come from the trade press, not from users.

### 1.5 Proposed rules

Per working hour, the worst state wins:

| Rule | GO | CAUTION | NO-GO | User-set? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gust at roof height | < 80% of limit | ≥ 80% of limit | > limit | **Yes.** Limit required. Suggest 35 mph labelled "UK trade guidance", or no default |
| Thunderstorm | none forecast | thunder probability above a threshold (number to choose; no source gives one) | weather code 95–99, or NWS thunder probability at or above a threshold | On by default. Locked on? (open question) |
| Rain | chance < 50% | chance ≥ 50% or measurable rain forecast (ENGINE §9 value; not sourced) | — | Yes, a toggle |
| Material minimum temperature | ≥ min + 5°F | within 5°F of min (margin is a proposal) | < min | **Yes**: pick a material, e.g. "Shingles 40°F (Malarkey)", "Self-adhered 40°F", or a custom value |
| Heat index (computed) | < 80°F | 80–103°F, shown with the NWS band word ("Caution", "Extreme Caution") | ≥ 103°F ("Danger"). Owner to decide whether this is NO-GO or CAUTION | Toggle. Thresholds from NWS |
| Freezing or frost | > 36°F | ≤ 36°F, or dew point within 2°F of temperature at ≤ 40°F (margins are proposals) | — | Toggle |
| Official alert | — | Wind Advisory, Heat Advisory | High Wind Warning, Severe Thunderstorm Warning (proposal) | No |
| Missing data | — | — | — | Any enabled input missing → `UNKNOWN` for that hour |

Wording: "Clear to work until 2 PM", "Caution: 38°F, below your shingle seal temperature", "No-go: storms forecast".

### 1.6 Open questions for the owner or users

1. Do roofers stop on gusts or on sustained wind? And is it the same limit for laying sheets as for shingling?
2. Is the material temperature rule a verdict (caution) or a note ("hand-seal today")? A roofer may still work but change method.
3. Should a thunderstorm forecast be NO-GO or CAUTION? A forecast "chance of storms" covers the whole afternoon. A NO-GO would block many summer days.
4. Should heat above 103°F be NO-GO, or a caution with a rest-break reminder?
5. How long after rain is a roof workable? We found no source, and roofers will have their own rule.
6. Residential, commercial low-slope, or both? The material lists differ.

## 2. Recreational boating (small craft)

### 2.1 What decides it, ranked

1. **Official marine alerts**: Small Craft Advisory, Gale, Storm, Hurricane Force Wind, Special Marine Warning.
2. **Sustained wind in knots**, then gusts.
3. **Seas or waves**: height, and period (short period means steep, uncomfortable seas).
4. **Thunderstorms or lightning.** A boat on open water is exposed.
5. **Wind direction against shore and against the tide.** Wind against tide makes water rougher than the
   forecast suggests ([Boat-Ed](https://www.boat-ed.com/blog/whats-a-marine-warning/)).
6. **Visibility** (fog).
7. **Water temperature** (cold-water risk). The Coast Guard says cool water drains body heat much faster
   than air, and to postpone trips when wind is high and temperatures are dropping
   ([Military.com on USCG District 9](https://www.military.com/daily-news/2015/12/22/coast-guard-warns-of-dangerous-water-temperatures.html)).
8. **Daylight.**

### 2.2 Thresholds

| Variable | Threshold | Source | Hardness |
|---|---|---|---|

"Small craft" has no precise definition | [NWS JAX glossary](https://www.weather.gov/jax/marine_glossary); [NWS PQR criteria](https://www.weather.gov/media/pqr/WFO%20PQR%20Marine%20and%20Coastal%20Hazard%20Criteria.pdf); [NWS directive 10-310 (2003)](https://www.weather.gov/directives/sym/pd01003010s042003curr.pdf) | Official |

**The user enters**: maximum sustained wind (kt), maximum gust (kt), maximum wave height (ft), and
optionally a minimum wave period. These depend on the boat and the skipper. Do not pre-fill them by boat size,
because no source supports that.

### 2.3 Units and wording

Knots for wind. Feet for seas. Seconds for period. Compass points for direction ("SW 15 to 20 kt").
"Seas 3 to 5 ft". "Chop", "a light chop", "rough" are NWS wording for bays and inland waters
(**unverified**). The NWS marine forecast format is the model users know.

| Rule | GO | CAUTION | NO-GO | User-set? |

Wording: "Go out until 1 PM: SW 8–12 kt, seas 1–2 ft". "Small Craft Advisory 1 PM–8 PM". "No-go: Gale Warning".

### 2.6 Open questions

1. Who is the user? A center-console owner offshore, a lake pontoon owner, a kayaker, or a sailor? The data quality is very different in each case.
2. Should a Small Craft Advisory be NO-GO by default? For a 16 ft skiff it probably is. For a 40 ft cruiser it may not be.
3. Does wind direction matter enough to set a per-place rule ("onshore over 15 kt")?
4. Should the app use tide and current at all in the verdict, or just show them?
5. What does a boater do with "Unknown waves" on a lake: trust wind only, or see no verdict?
6. Liability. A boating "GO" is riskier to give than a roofing one. How strong should the disclaimer be?

## 3. Golf

### 3.1 What decides it, ranked

1. **Lightning.** It is the only weather hazard the Rules of Golf deal with directly.
2. **Rain**: amount and duration, plus course status (closed, or carts on paths only).
3. **Heat index.**
4. **Wind**: comfort and scoring, rarely safety until very high.
5. **Frost** (morning delays). **Cold.**
6. **Daylight** (finishing the round).

### 3.2 Thresholds

| Variable | Threshold | Source | Hardness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lightning | A player may stop on their own if they reasonably believe lightning is a danger (Rule 5.7a). When the Committee suspends play for danger, play stops at once. Signals: one long horn note = immediate suspension; three notes = normal suspension; two short notes = resume | [USGA rules notebook](https://www.usga.org/content/usga/home-page/rules-hub/2019-rules-notebook--open-championships/evacuation-suspending-resuming-play.html); [UGA Rule 5 Q&A](https://www.uga.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/11/2019/01/Rule-5-Questions-and-Answers.pdf) | Rules of the game. They apply to competitions. Courses set their own policy |
| Lightning, general | Go indoors at thunder. Stay until 30 min after the last thunder | [NOAA JetStream](https://noaa.gov/jetstream/lightning/lightning-safety) | Guidance |
| Wind | No standard. Example club policies: closed above 50 mph average or Beaufort 9 (Dumbarnie Links); gusts above 40 mph or gale force 8 trigger closure, 25–38 mph means holes may close (Broomieknowe, UK); play suspended when gusts exceed 62 km/h (Geelong, AU) | [Dumbarnie](https://www.dumbarnielinks.com/bad-weather-policy/); [Broomieknowe](https://broomieknowe.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/HS02-Course-closure-in-high-winds-policy.pdf); [Geelong](https://www.geelonggolf.com.au/golf-course/extreme-weather-policies/) | Club policy |

Everything except lightning is comfort.

### 3.3 Units and wording

mph and °F (US). "Tee time", "rain delay", "frost delay", "cart path only", "playable". The app cannot know
course status. It must not say "the course is open".

### 3.4 Data availability

| Variable | Status |
|---|---|
| Wind, gust, rain, temperature | Available |
| Heat index | Partly (compute it) |
| Thunderstorm forecast | Partly (forecast only) |
| **Live lightning distance**, the thing golfers and courses actually use | **Not available.** Paid feed, deferred (DATA_SOURCES.md) |
| Frost | Partly. Inferred from low temperature and dew point |
| Course closure, cart rules | Not available |
| Daylight | Available |

### 3.5 Proposed rules

| Rule | GO | CAUTION | NO-GO | User-set? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Thunderstorm | none forecast | thunder probability above a threshold: "Storms possible" | weather code 95–99: "Storms likely" | On, cannot be turned off (proposal) |
| Wind (mph, sustained or gust; user chooses) | < 80% | ≥ 80% | > limit | Yes |
| Rain | chance < user value | chance ≥ user value | precipitation above a user amount (proposal) | Yes |
| Heat index | < user limit | ≥ user limit, with NWS band word | ≥ 103°F (proposal) | Yes |
| Frost possible (early hours) | — | temperature ≤ 36°F and near dew point: "Frost delay possible" (proposal) | — | Toggle |
| Daylight | — | round would end after sunset (needs round length, default 4 h? **no source**) | — | Toggle |

Wording: "Good to play 8 AM–1 PM". "Storms possible after 2 PM". Never say "safe from lightning".

### 3.6 Open questions

1. Is golf a "work" activity at all? Course superintendents and staff (frost, mowing, spraying) may be a better user than players.
2. Without live lightning, is a golf verdict useful enough? Players can already see "chance of storms" in any weather app.
3. Would anyone pay for this?

## 4. Drone flying (small UAS)

### 4.1 What decides it, ranked

1. **Wind at flight height**, especially gusts, against the drone's rated limit.
2. **Rain or snow.** Most consumer drones have no water-resistance rating.
3. **Visibility and cloud ceiling.** Legal limits under Part 107.
4. **Thunderstorms.**
5. **Temperature.** Battery performance and the operating range.
6. **Daylight or twilight.** Lighting and training rules apply.
7. **Geomagnetic activity (Kp).** GPS reliability; a minor factor.

### 4.2 Thresholds

| Recreational altitude | 400 ft in Class G airspace. TRUST test required | [FAA recreational flyers](https://faa.gov/uas/recreational_flyers); [49 USC 44809](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/49/44809) | Legal |

**The user enters**: drone model, which sets the wind spec (offer a list plus "custom"). Also flight height
(default 400 ft), whether they fly under Part 107 or recreationally (this decides whether visibility and
ceiling are legal limits or advice), and whether they have night lighting.

### 4.3 Units and wording

Pilots see m/s on DJI spec sheets, mph or km/h in the DJI Fly app (**unverified**), and knots in aviation weather
(METAR/TAF). Offer all three, defaulting to mph in the US. Visibility in statute miles ("3 SM"). Ceiling in ft AGL.
Part 107 pilots use "VLOS", "AGL", "ceiling", "Kp".

### 4.4 Data availability

| Variable | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Wind at 80 m and 120 m (~400 ft) | Open-Meteo `wind_speed_80m`, `wind_speed_120m` (also 180 m) | **Available, direct.** Strong fit. Gusts are only at 10 m: scale them by the 120 m / 10 m wind ratio (ENGINE §2) |
| Wind at height (NWS) | Power law only | Partly |
| Visibility | Open-Meteo `visibility` (model-dependent; not in every model); NWS `visibility` layer (**unverified**) | Partly |
| Cloud ceiling | NWS `ceilingHeight` layer (mirror only, **unverified**). Open-Meteo has cloud cover by level but no ceiling field we could confirm | **Partly / unverified.** Key gap for Part 107 |
| Rain, snow | Both | Available |
| Thunderstorm | weather code, CAPE, NWS | Partly |
| Temperature | Both | Available |
| Civil twilight | Calculated | Available |
| Kp | NOAA SWPC | Not in our current sources. Free feed exists (SWPC, recalled, **unverified** format) |
| Airspace, TFRs | FAA | **Not weather, out of scope.** Users need a separate app (B4UFLY, LAANC) |

### 4.5 Proposed rules

| Rule | GO | CAUTION | NO-GO | User-set? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gust at flight height vs drone spec | < 80% of spec | ≥ 80% | > spec | **Yes**: model picker, or the user's own lower limit |
| Precipitation | chance < 30% (proposal) | chance ≥ 30% or any forecast rain | measurable rain, unless the drone is marked water-resistant | Toggle; IP flag per drone |
| Visibility (Part 107) | ≥ 3 SM + margin | 3–4 SM (proposal) | < 3 SM, labelled "below the Part 107 minimum" | Legal value; switch to advice when recreational |
| Ceiling (Part 107) | ≥ flight height + 500 ft + margin | within margin | < flight height + 500 ft, labelled "Part 107 cloud clearance" | Same. `UNKNOWN` if no ceiling data |
| Thunderstorm | — | storms possible | storms likely (weather code 95–99) | On |
| Temperature | inside the operating range | within 5°C of the range limit, or < 10°C ("warm batteries") | outside the range | From the model |
| Twilight / night | daylight | civil twilight: "lighting required" | night with no lighting or training | Yes |
| Kp | < 5 | ≥ 5 | — | Toggle, off by default |

Wording: "Clear to fly until 3 PM: gusts at 400 ft 14–19 mph (your Mini 4 Pro: 24)". "Caution: ceiling 700 ft, flying at 400".

### 4.6 Open questions

1. Who is the user: commercial Part 107 (roof inspection, real estate, survey) or hobby? Commercial users fit "work" better.
2. Does the 80% caution make sense against a spec DJI itself calls a wind-tunnel figure? Pilots may want a much lower personal limit.
3. Is the Part 107 cloud clearance worth showing without reliable ceiling data?
4. Free competitors exist (UAV Forecast is well known; we did not research it). What makes ours worth switching to?
5. Should airspace be linked out to, or left alone?

## 5. Comparison with crane

Crane (ENGINE.md) has one hard limit: the machine's rated wind, measured as a gust at boom height against the user's number.

- **Drone** is the closest match: a manufacturer spec, wind at height, a user picks their machine. It adds legal visibility and ceiling.
- **Roofing** is the same gust engine with low height, plus material temperature rules.
- **Boating** swaps height for waves and official marine alerts. Its data is the hardest.
- **Golf** has no wind limit that matters for safety. Its real limit, lightning, needs data we do not have.

## 6. Second launch activity

**Recommendation: roofing, then drone.**

**Roofing**
- Every input is available from both sources today.
- The engine needs only ENGINE §9's extra rules, plus a material temperature table.
- The product already names it as the proposed launch pair.
- It is paid work with a daily decision, and it shares the work-at-height audience with crane.
- Weaknesses: no authoritative wind number (we must make the limit user-set, as with crane), and competitors
  already target roofing (PRODUCT.md lists DecideWeather, CanWork and others).

**Drone, a close second**
- Best data fit for wind at height: the 120 m wind matches the 400 ft ceiling.
- Clear legal limits and manufacturer specs give users credible numbers.
- Commercial Part 107 pilots are paid workers.
- Weaknesses: cloud ceiling is unverified in both sources, free drone weather apps already exist, and airspace (not weather) is often what blocks a flight.

**Boating: later**
- Wave forecasts are unreliable on bays, lakes and rivers, where many small boats operate.
- Advisory criteria differ by region, so the defaults cannot be national.
- A wrong "GO" carries the most risk.
- It needs marine zones, tides and currents: three more integrations.

**Golf: last**
- The deciding hazard is live lightning, which we have deferred.
- The rest is comfort, and generic weather apps already cover it.
- Weakest fit with "work".

**Applies to all**: Open-Meteo's free API is for non-commercial use only. Commercial use requires a paid
subscription ([Open-Meteo terms](https://open-meteo.com/en/terms); [pricing](https://open-meteo.com/en/pricing)).
Drone and boating depend most on Open-Meteo-only fields (120 m wind, waves), so the licence matters most there.

## 7. What to verify first

1. Pull one NWS gridpoint response for an inland site and one for a coastal site. Confirm `probabilityOfThunder`, `ceilingHeight`, `visibility`, `heatIndex`, `waveHeight`, and the units.
2. Pull Open-Meteo forecast and marine responses for the same points. Confirm `visibility` and `dew_point_2m` for the default US model, whether a ceiling or cloud-base field exists, and the knots unit option.
3. Read the primary texts: 14 CFR 107.49 and 107.51 on eCFR, OSHA FS-3863, 1926.451(f)(12), the NWS directive 10-310 current version and the local office SCA criteria for target regions.
4. Collect the material temperature minimums from GAF, Owens Corning and CertainTeed installation instructions for the shingles target users install.
5. Check the alert API's `point=` filter and marine zone lookup.
